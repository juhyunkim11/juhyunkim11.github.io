00 — Thesis

The break in the rule is the finding.

Every energy actor took the same shock in 2020 and walked away with a different future. That asymmetry is what sent me toward business.

When the COVID-19 pandemic collapsed global oil demand, Russia and Saudi Arabia dug in. The European Union used the fall in its import bill to accelerate a transition it had already wanted. Same event, opposite trajectories. Writing that paper taught me the habit I now bring to everything: find the case where the obvious rule breaks, and ask what the break is telling you.

I did it again in marketing, co-authoring a study on celebrity endorsement that ran against the field's own Fit Hypothesis. We ran 32 participants across six real endorsement pairings and found that a deliberate mismatch, like Shaquille O'Neal for Taco Bell, can outperform a tidy one.

Then I stopped writing about markets and built into one. HoopTrust is a recruiting pipeline for international student-athletes who are good enough for U.S. college basketball and invisible to the people who do the recruiting. It uses computer vision to pull verified performance metrics out of raw game footage, so a player in Jeju can be scouted the way a player in Texas already is.

Away from that I practise piano, and I captain varsity baseball and start for varsity basketball. The three are not separate habits. Research, a venture and a scale all reward the same thing: unglamorous repetition, long before anyone is watching.
