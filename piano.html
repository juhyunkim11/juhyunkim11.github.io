The practice

The part that happens before anyone claps.

I am a classical pianist first. I hold the ABRSM Grade 8 with Distinction — the highest-level graded exam from the Associated Board of the Royal Schools of Music, and the clearest single measure of technical and musical proficiency.

Competition piano is mostly an argument with yourself in an empty room. You play a passage that is nearly right, and nearly right is the problem, so you play it again. Then once more, slower. The concert is forty minutes long and it is the smallest part of the work by an enormous margin.

Walking out at Carnegie Hall in 2025 and at Lincoln Center a year later did not feel like an arrival so much as a receipt — evidence that the hours had been spent. That is also, I have come to think, why the discipline transfers. A research paper and a startup pitch both end in a room with an audience and both are decided long before you get there.

While I primarily perform as a solo classical pianist, playing jazz with friends offers something outside competition. At every music festival we play together — no scoring, no jury, an audience of whoever happens to be nearby. We have played the school dormitory, cafés and restaurants, and straight off the street for local residents. Busking teaches something a hall cannot: if the playing is not good, people simply keep walking.
