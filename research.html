Paper 01 · Political economy

Asymmetric Critical Junctures: The Political Economy of the COVID-19 Energy Demand Shock

The 2020 pandemic was not a normal energy crisis. Where 1973 and 2014 were supply shocks driven by embargoes and new production, COVID-19 produced a sudden, policy-induced collapse in demand — roughly a 9% fall in global oil consumption caused by public health mandates rather than by any physical constraint. This paper argues that the shock hit exporters and importers asymmetrically. Russia and Saudi Arabia absorbed a double hit of collapsing volume and record low prices, which reinforced fossil fuel dependence and coordination pressure among producers. For importers like the European Union, the same event arrived as a fiscal windfall that cut import bills and weakened the case for protecting domestic fossil industries. Tracing four actors — Russia, Saudi Arabia, the EU and China as a pragmatic transition leader — the paper shows how structural position, not the shock itself, determined each actor's policy space and produced a long-term divergence in climate trajectories.

Paper 02 · Consumer behaviour

The Power of Mismatch: How Incongruous Pairings Drive Ad Effectiveness

Celebrity endorsement has long run on the Fit Hypothesis: the closer the match between celebrity and product, the more trust and expertise transfer to the brand. This study tests that assumption. Thirty-two participants evaluated six real endorsement pairings against three fit metrics — expertise, image congruence and audience overlap — and three success metrics — brand recall, image improvement and interest change. High-fit endorsements did rate strongest overall, but not every low-fit pairing failed. Shaquille O'Neal for Taco Bell showed how emotional appeal and social proof can compensate for weak fit. A thematic analysis of 192 written justifications surfaced five drivers of endorsement success: credibility and social proof, emotional and creative appeal, involvement and personal relevance, mismatch and tension, and product-celebrity congruence. The conclusion is not that fit is wrong, but that strategic incongruity is a second, under-used route to attention — one that trades long-term trust for humour, novelty and creative surprise.

Co-authored with Ayden Kim · both authors contributed equally
Independent researcher under Professor Kit Hickey, MIT
Volume 1, Issue 2 · Summer 2025
