The problem

Recruiting runs on trust, and trust does not travel.

A U.S. college coach recruiting domestically has a whole infrastructure standing behind every number they read: circuit games they can attend, scouts they know, film they can cross-check. None of that reaches an international player. The stats a player in Korea or Nigeria or Lithuania submits are self-reported, unverifiable, and therefore discounted to near zero by the person reading them.

So the bottleneck is not talent and it is not footage. Phones have solved footage. The bottleneck is credibility — nobody can afford to send a human scout to watch a player they have no reason to believe in yet, which means the player never gets the look that would make them believable.

That is a classic blue ocean: the existing market competes hard over the players everyone can already see, and ignores a far larger group of non-customers who are shut out by the cost structure rather than by ability.

The 24/7 digital scout

  • Extracts over 15 verified performance metrics straight from raw game footage, so the number and the evidence for the number arrive together.
  • Removes human bias from the record. A profile is not a claim a player makes about themselves; it is a claim the footage supports.
  • Produces a per-profile trust score that tells a recruiter exactly how much of what they are reading has been checked.

Edge AI, because the unit economics have to work

ArchitectureEdge inference

Why it can be cheap

  • Processing video in the cloud is what makes existing analysis tools expensive enough to price out the exact families the product exists for.
  • Moving inference to the edge cuts server overhead by roughly 60%, which is what makes a $29/month subscription viable rather than aspirational.
  • The cost structure is the strategy. A disruptive model only counts as disruptive if the non-customer can actually afford to become a customer.
